Why freelancers use QuickInvoice

  • Project-based or hourly billing — list a flat project fee, or break work into hourly line items with quantity as hours worked.
  • Fast turnaround — send an invoice the moment a project wraps, from your phone if needed.
  • Consistent client billing — reuse the same business details and template across every client, so your invoices look consistent.
  • Clear payment terms — set Net 7, Net 14, Net 30 or a custom due date so clients know exactly when payment is expected.
  • A professional first impression — a well-formatted invoice signals you run your freelance work like a real business.

What to put on a freelance invoice

Freelance invoices work best when scope is specific — vague line items lead to payment delays and disputes.

  • "Website redesign — homepage and 3 inner pages" — Qty 1, flat project rate
  • "Copywriting — blog posts" — Qty 4, price per post
  • "Consulting call" — Qty 2 (hours), hourly rate

Tips for getting paid faster as a freelancer

  • State your payment terms up front, ideally in your project agreement and again on the invoice.
  • Invoice promptly — the longer you wait after finishing work, the longer clients tend to take to pay.
  • Keep a consistent invoice numbering system so you and your client can reference past invoices easily.
  • If you bill hourly, keep your line items specific ("Homepage revisions — 2 hrs") rather than a single lump sum.

Frequently asked questions

Can I invoice for a flat project fee instead of by the hour?

Yes — just add a single line item with quantity 1 and your project price, or break it into milestones as separate line items.

Can I set different payment terms for different clients?

Yes, the due date and payment terms are editable per invoice, so you can offer Net 7 to one client and Net 30 to another.

Do I need a business registration to use this?

No — QuickInvoice works for sole proprietors, registered businesses, and anyone freelancing under their own name.

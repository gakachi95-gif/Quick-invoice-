Invoice Generator for Cleaning Services

Cleaning businesses often juggle one-off jobs, recurring visits, and multiple properties for the same client. QuickInvoice keeps each invoice simple to create, whether you're billing per visit or for a full month of service.

Create a Free Invoice

Why cleaning businesses use QuickInvoice

  • Per-visit or monthly billing — list each cleaning date as a line item, or bundle a month of visits into one invoice.
  • Supplies vs. labor — separate a cleaning supplies fee from your labor charge if you don't bundle them into one rate.
  • Multiple properties — the customer address field makes it clear which property an invoice is for, useful if you clean more than one location for the same client.
  • Fast on mobile — create and send an invoice from your phone right after a job, without needing a laptop.
  • Simple recurring pattern — reuse the same business details and template for every recurring client.

What to put on a cleaning invoice

Clear line items help recurring clients understand what a monthly total covers.

  • "Standard clean — 3-bed house" — Qty 4 (visits this month), price per visit
  • "Deep clean add-on — oven and windows" — Qty 1, flat fee
  • "Cleaning supplies" — Qty 1, supplies fee

Tips for cleaning service invoicing

  • If you bill monthly, list each visit date as its own line item so clients can see exactly what they paid for.
  • State your cancellation or reschedule policy in the Terms & Conditions field to avoid disputes.
  • Keep the customer address on the invoice if you service multiple properties for the same client.
  • Send invoices on a consistent schedule (e.g. the 1st of each month) so recurring clients know what to expect.

Frequently asked questions

Can I bill for a whole month of cleanings on one invoice?

Yes — add each visit as a separate line item, or a single line item covering the full month.

Can I list the property address on the invoice?

Yes, the customer address field is designed for exactly that.

Can I charge a separate supplies fee?

Yes — add it as its own line item alongside your labor charge.
